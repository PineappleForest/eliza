## Facts (with sources)
- John Gray died of tuberculosis on 15 February 1858 and was buried in Greyfriars Kirkyard, Edinburgh. His Skye
  terrier Bobby is said to have stayed by the grave for 14 years, until the dog's death on 14 January 1872.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyfriars_Bobby ; https://www.historyhit.com/greyfriars-bobby-scotlands-most-famous-dog/
- He refused to leave the grave "even in the worst weather conditions" (https://go.alt.ac.uk/2YbmocJ).
- Locals cared for him (History Hit; The Vintage News).
- 1867: the Lord Provost, Sir William Chambers, paid his dog licence and gave him a collar inscribed
  "Greyfriars Bobby from the Lord Provost 1867 licensed" (Historic UK: https://historic-uk.com/HistoryUK/HistoryofScotland/Greyfriars-Bobby).
- Statue/fountain by William Brodie, 1872-73, funded by Baroness Burdett-Coutts.
- Caveat: historians have questioned details of the tale (Greater Good; Questo), so the script says "is said to".
