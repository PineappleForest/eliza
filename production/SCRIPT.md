## Facts and sources
1. Weizenbaum built ELIZA at MIT (1964-67); DOCTOR imitated a Rogerian therapist by reflecting the user's words back as questions. — Wired/MIT Press "Inventing ELIZA" via aiweekly.co; 99percentinvisible.org
2. He chose a psychiatrist because one party "doesn't have to know everything" (Weizenbaum, 1984). — mentalfloss.com
3. His secretary watched him build it for months, knew it was a program, typed a few sentences, then asked him to leave the room. — Weizenbaum (1967: 477) via elizaarchaeology; open.edu
4. Users revealed intimate details about their lives. — 99percentinvisible.org
5. Many psychiatrists believed it could become nearly completely automatic psychotherapy. — Weizenbaum, Computer Power and Human Reason (1976)
6. Carl Sagan (1975) imagined psychotherapeutic terminals "something like arrays of large telephone booths". — grandtextauto.soe.ucsc.edu
7. ELIZA kept nothing between sessions (no persistent memory). — ELIZA (1966 CACM) design; original code restored 2024-25, arXiv:2501.06707
8. The phenomenon was named the "ELIZA effect". — history.com; Wikipedia ELIZA

## Script
1. In 1966, a computer program got its own inventor asked to leave the room.
2. MIT. Joseph Weizenbaum built ELIZA to play a therapist, because a therapist can just ask questions.
3. His secretary had watched him build it for months. She knew exactly what it was.
4. She typed a few lines, turned around, and asked him to leave.
5. Visitors poured out their secrets. Psychiatrists predicted fully automatic therapy. Carl Sagan pictured rows of therapy booths, like phone booths.
6. But ELIZA had a problem. Close the session, and it forgot you ever existed.
7. Luckily, it never judged you. Unluckily, it never remembered you either.
8. Here's the trick. ELIZA was a mirror. "I'm sad" became "Why are you sad?"
9. Like talking to your dog. He doesn't follow a word, but he looks at you like he does, so you keep talking.
10. It got a name: the ELIZA effect.
11. Sixty years later, Feeloy listens, and remembers. Your day, your people, the little things.
12. ELIZA got its inventor sent out of the room. Feeloy just asks how your interview went.
13. Search Feeloy on the App Store.
