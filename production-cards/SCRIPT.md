# "100 Cards" (Joe Cuba's 100th birthday -> Feeloy)

Composition (to build once the take arrives): `CardsShort`, code `src/videos/cards/`, output `out/cards_final.mp4`.
Narration: ElevenLabs, voice "Mark". 150 words (Mark reads ~2.75 words/s; several spelled-out numbers -> expect ~58-62 s).

## Facts (with sources)
- Joe Cuba, a WWII Army Air Forces veteran living at Brookdale Midwestern, an assisted living community in Wichita Falls,
  Texas, turned 100 on 2 March 2019.
  - https://abcnews.go.com/living/story/wwii-veteran-asked-100-birthday-cards-100th-birthday-61391343
- A caregiver (Stephanie Veitenheimer) asked what he wanted; he said "Nothing really, just happiness." She photographed him
  with a posterboard: "I'm a WW2 veteran who will be turning 100 on March 2, 2019. I would like to receive 100 birthday cards."
  - https://www.ksl.com/article/news/features/uplifting/wwii-vet-asks-for-100-cards-on-100th-birthday-receives-thousands-instead/46501769
- Posted 21 Feb 2019; by ~26 Feb more than 2,500 cards and 100+ gifts.
  - https://abc13.com/post/veteran-asks-for-100-birthday-cards-but-gets-thousands/5158536/
- As of 28 Feb, more than 16,000 letters, cards, gifts and flowers, filling tables and floors of an empty apartment; cards
  from Japan, Germany, Australia, Puerto Rico, the UK and Guam.
  - https://www.ksl.com/article/news/features/uplifting/wwii-vet-asks-for-100-cards-on-100th-birthday-receives-thousands-instead/46501769
- One card from Hong Kong was express-mailed at a cost of US$300 so it would arrive in time; staff opened a vacant room
  for the greetings; by the birthday party, 50,000+ cards.
  - https://globalnews.ca/news/5019682/wwii-veteran-50000-cards-100th-birthday
  - https://www.koaa.com/news/2019/03/04/wwii-vet-gets-thousands-of-birthday-cards
- Asked whether he got his 100 cards, he joked "I bet I have."
  - https://www.ktvu.com/news/texas-wwii-veteran-receives-thousands-of-cards-for-his-100th-birthday
- Joe is a real person: drawn as a warm, generic, very old man (cardigan, cap), never mocked. No real logos, no real
  facility name, no uniforms or insignia beyond a plain cap.

## Script (150 words)
1. A man turning one hundred asked for one hundred birthday cards.
2. The mailman was not ready.
3. Joe was living in a care home in Texas.
4. When a caregiver asked what he wanted, he said: nothing really. Just happiness.
5. So she took his photo with a sign: I would like to receive one hundred birthday cards.
6. Within days: two thousand five hundred cards.
7. Then sixteen thousand. From Japan. Germany. Australia. Guam.
8. Someone paid three hundred dollars to express-mail one card from Hong Kong.
9. The home had to open an empty room just for the mail.
10. On his birthday: fifty thousand cards.
11. Did he get his hundred? "I bet I have."
12. He asked for so little. Just to be thought of.
13. Not everyone has fifty thousand strangers writing back.
14. That's why we made Feeloy. Jenny is always there, remembers you, and writes back, any time.
15. Search Feeloy on the App Store.

## Shot plan (draft, timed to the take later)
1. Hook: Joe in his armchair holding the hand-written sign "100 CARDS PLEASE"; a big "100" pops.
2. The mailman on the path outside, a sack on his back, staggering; sweat drop; it gets heavier.
3. Establishing: a low care home on open grass, golden light, a mailbox at the gate.
4. Inside: a caregiver kneels by Joe's chair; thought bubble with a heart/"note" icon ("just happiness").
5. Phone insert: she snaps the photo; the photo flies off into a share burst.
6. Counter card "2,500" over a table piled with envelopes.
7. Counter "16,000"; world map card with stamps popping in (Japan, Germany, Australia, Guam).
8. One glowing envelope flies across, a "$300" tag and an express sticker.
9. An empty room fills with mail: a door opens, envelopes stack to the ceiling.
10. Birthday: cake with candles, the counter rolls to "50,000", confetti.
11. Close-up of Joe, deadpan wink: "I bet I have."
12. Quiet: Joe reading one card in lamplight.
13. A lonely person at night in a city flat, an empty mailbox.
14. Phone insert: Jenny, a reply bubble, a heart.
15. End card.
