# "Call Sweden" (The Swedish Number, 2016 -> Feeloy)

Composition (to build once the take arrives): `SwedenShort`, code `src/videos/sweden/`, output `out/sweden_final.mp4`.
Narration: ElevenLabs, voice "Mark". ~140 words (Mark reads ~2.75 words/s -> ~58-62 s with the pauses).

## Facts (with sources)
- April 2016: the Swedish Tourist Association gave Sweden "its own phone number" (+46 771 793 336). Calling it from
  abroad connected you to a random Swede; you could talk about anything.
  - https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2016/04/07/473438101/call-a-random-swede-we-tried-it-out
  - https://www.lovethework.com/work-awards/entries/54782
- The Swedes who answered volunteered through an app; they were not vetted or given instructions on what to say.
  - https://globalnews.ca/news/2624817/sweden-invites-world-to-call-random-swedish-citizens-on-new-hotline
- Suggested topics included meatballs, darkness and feminism.
  - https://www.npr.org/sections/thetwo-way/2016/04/07/473438101/call-a-random-swede-we-tried-it-out
- Results: 188,625 calls (Wikipedia reports 197,678), 186 countries calling, accumulated calling time 376 days,
  longest call 4 h 42 min.
  - https://www2.eurobest.com/winners/2016/pr
  - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Swedish_Number
- The callers, the Swedes and the long-call caller are generic illustrative characters. The "maybe someone just
  needed a person to pick up" line is the narrator wondering, not a reported fact. No brand logos.

## Script (~140 words)
1. In twenty-sixteen, Sweden got its own phone number.
2. Call it from anywhere in the world... and a random Swede picks up.
3. No script. No instructions. Just a stranger, saying "hej".
4. Suggested topics? Meatballs. Darkness. Feminism.
5. People called from one hundred and eighty-six countries.
6. Nearly two hundred thousand calls.
7. Add them all up, and the world talked to Sweden for three hundred and seventy-six days.
8. The longest call lasted four hours... and forty-two minutes.
9. Maybe that wasn't really about meatballs.
10. Maybe someone just needed a person to pick up.
11. Most of us don't have a whole country on speed dial.
12. That's why we made Feeloy. Jenny always picks up, remembers what you told her, and never runs out of time to talk.
13. Search Feeloy on the App Store.

## Shot plan (draft, timed to the take later)
1. Hook: a giant old red telephone on a snowy Swedish hillside with a little red cabin; it rings.
2. A globe spins; a caller in a city dials; the call line arcs over the map to Sweden.
3. A Swede in a cosy kitchen answers, a little surprised: "Hej!"
4. Three quick inserts above the Swede: a plate of meatballs, a dark window with stars, a pair of equal-sign speech bubbles.
5. The map lights up with 186 dots.
6. A counter on a big switchboard ticks to nearly 200,000.
7. A calendar flipping pages: 376 days.
8. A wall clock spinning; the Swede still on the phone, the caller with tea going cold; 4:42 on a timer.
9. Close on the caller far away, alone at night, smiling into the phone.
10. Same caller, the call ends; the room goes quiet.
11. A phone with no "Sweden" button.
12. Phone insert: Jenny picks up, a note bubble, a heart.
13. End card.
