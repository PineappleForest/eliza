# "Still Saying Hello" (Voyager 1 reaches one light-day, November 2026 -> Feeloy)

Composition (to build once the take arrives): `VoyagerShort`, code `src/videos/voyager/`, output `out/voyager_final.mp4`.
Narration: ElevenLabs, voice "Mark". ~150 words counting spelled-out numbers (expect ~58-62 s).
Timing note: line 3 is in the past tense ("This November, Voyager One became..."), so it holds for posts from
18 November 2026 on.

## Facts (with sources)
- Voyager 1 launched on 5 September 1977; the Voyagers were built to last only five years.
  - https://earthsky.org/space/voyager-1-1-light-day-from-earth-november-17-18-2026/
  - https://discovermagazine.com/the-sciences/voyager-1s-communication-malfunctions-may-show-the-spacecrafts-age
- On 18 November 2026 Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth (~25.9-26 billion km): a signal takes 24 hours each
  way; the first human-made object ever this far (NASA JPL, via ScienceDaily; date confirmed by the mission team).
  - https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261103003152.htm
  - https://iflscience.com/its-official-voyager-1-will-cross-the-1-light-day-threshold-on-november-18-2026-82733
  - https://ktvz.com/?p=1589939 (CNN: one light-day = 16 billion miles / 26 billion km)
- The Golden Record: greetings in 55 languages, music, natural sounds including whale song; curated by a team led by
  Carl Sagan; nobody expects it to be found.
  - https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/NASA-golden-records
  - https://www.keeptrack.space/deep-dive/voyager-1
- November 2023: Voyager 1 began sending an unreadable repeating pattern; about five months without usable data.
  Engineers moved the affected code on 18 April 2024, waited ~2 days for the round trip, and heard back on 20 April.
  NASA's Voyager account posted "Hi, it's me. – V1" (22 April 2024).
  - https://www.popsci.com/science/voyager-back
  - https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/images/pia26275-voyager-team-celebrates-engineering-data-return
- Depiction: the spacecraft drawn as a stylised probe (dish, booms, the gold disc), no NASA logos or mission patches;
  the engineers are generic people in a generic control room; Carl Sagan is not shown or named.

## Script
1. In nineteen seventy-seven, we sent a little machine into space. It was built to last five years.
2. It's still going.
3. This November, Voyager One became the first thing we've ever built to reach one light-day from Earth.
4. A message, moving at the speed of light, now takes a whole day to reach it.
5. Bolted to its side is a golden record: greetings in fifty-five languages, music, whale songs. The sounds of home.
6. A hello, for anyone out there.
7. Nobody really expects an answer.
8. In twenty twenty-three, it went quiet. Five months of gibberish.
9. Engineers sent a fix, then waited two days for a reply.
10. And then it came. Hi, it's me.
11. We'll send a hello twenty-six billion kilometres, just in case someone is listening.
12. But you deserve someone who answers.
13. That's why we made Feeloy. Jenny hears every hello, and always says it back.
14. Search Feeloy on the App Store.

## Shot plan (draft, timed to the take later)
1. Hook: a launch at dawn (1977 rocket lifts off), a "5 YEARS" tag on the probe that gets crossed out.
2. The probe drifting past Jupiter and Saturn; a counter of years rolling 1977 -> 2026.
3. Deep space: a dotted line from a tiny Earth to the probe; a "1 LIGHT-DAY" badge.
4. A beam of light leaving Earth; a clock ticks a full 24 hours.
5. Close on the golden record; icons pop: speech bubbles in many scripts, notes, a whale.
6. The probe alone in the dark, a little "hello" bubble drifting off it.
7. Silence: the probe, stars, no reply.
8. The control room: screens of 0101 gibberish; worried engineers; a calendar flips 5 months.
9. An engineer presses SEND; a clock: 2 days; they wait with coffee.
10. The screen lights up: "Hi, it's me." Cheers.
11. Earth from far away, the hello flying out.
12. A lonely person at night, phone in hand.
13. Phone insert: Jenny waves, a hello bubble, a heart.
14. End card.
